## 2.5.1 PD Classification Problem
*Use to define PD recognition as a supervised classification task: inputs (PRPD / φ-q-n, time-resolved pulses), output classes (void, surface, corona, floating, etc.), and the complications of noise and multiple sources.*

1. ★ **[R, seminal]** N. C. Sahoo, M. M. A. Salama, and R. Bartnikas, "Trends in partial discharge pattern classification: a survey," *IEEE Trans. Dielectr. Electr. Insul.*, vol. 12, no. 2, pp. 248–264, Apr. 2005, doi: [10.1109/TDEI.2005.1430395](https://doi.org/10.1109/TDEI.2005.1430395).
   Classic framing of PD recognition as a two-stage problem: feature extraction, then classification. Notes that multi-source patterns from real insulation remain hard to classify.
2. ★ **[P, seminal]** A. Krivda, "Automated recognition of partial discharges," *IEEE Trans. Dielectr. Electr. Insul.*, vol. 2, no. 5, pp. 796–821, Oct. 1995, doi: [10.1109/94.469976](https://doi.org/10.1109/94.469976).
   Defines the building blocks of an automatic recognition system: the PD pattern (φ-q-n), feature extraction, the reference database and the classifier.
3. **[P, seminal]** ⚠ E. Gulski, "Digital analysis of partial discharges," *IEEE Trans. Dielectr. Electr. Insul.*, vol. 2, no. 5, pp. 822–837, Oct. 1995.
   Phase-resolved distributions (H_qn, H_n(φ)) used as recognition inputs. *(Check the end page and DOI.)*
4. ★ **[S]** IEC 60270:2025, *High-voltage test techniques – Charge-based measurement of partial discharges*, 4th ed., IEC, 2025. (The previous edition was IEC 60270:2000+AMD1:2015; cite whichever edition the lab follows.)
   The formal definition of PD and apparent charge.
5. **[S]** CIGRE WG D1.37, *Guidelines for partial discharge detection using conventional (IEC 60270) and unconventional methods*, TB 662, CIGRE, 2016.
   Covers HFCT and UHF, the project's sensors, for cables.
6. **[R]** J. A. Ardila-Rey *et al.*, "Separation techniques of partial discharges and electrical noise sources: A review of recent progress," *IEEE Access*, vol. 8, pp. 199449–199461, 2020, doi: [10.1109/ACCESS.2020.3035249](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2020.3035249).
   Supports the point that multiple sources and noise must be separated before classification.
7. ★ **[R, recent, Cable]** H. Kumar, M. Shafiq, K. Kauhaniemi, and M. Elmusrati, "A review on the classification of partial discharges in medium-voltage cables: Detection, feature extraction, artificial intelligence-based classification, and optimization techniques," *Energies*, vol. 17, no. 5, Art. 1142, 2024, doi: [10.3390/en17051142](https://doi.org/10.3390/en17051142).
   The best single framing source for a cable PD classification pipeline.
8. **[R, recent, Cable]** ⚠ S. Govindarajan, A. Morales, J. A. Ardila-Rey, and N. Purushothaman, "A review on partial discharge diagnosis in cables: Theory, techniques, and trends," *Measurement*, vol. 216, Art. 112882, 2023, doi: [10.1016/j.measurement.2023.112882](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.measurement.2023.112882). *(Check author order.)*

## 2.5.2 Traditional Machine-Learning Approaches
*Hand-crafted features (statistical PRPD features such as skewness and kurtosis, fractal, wavelet and TF-map features) fed to classifiers (ANN/MLP, SVM, kNN, fuzzy, RF, K-means).*

1. ★ **[P, seminal]** ⚠ E. Gulski and A. Krivda, "Neural networks as a tool for recognition of partial discharges," *IEEE Trans. Electr. Insul.*, vol. 28, no. 6, pp. 984–1001, Dec. 1993, doi: [10.1109/14.249372](https://doi.org/10.1109/14.249372).
   Statistical φ-q-n descriptors (skewness, kurtosis, cross-correlation) fed to BP, SOM and LVQ networks: the origin of the statistical-feature approach.
2. **[P, seminal]** ⚠ L. Satish and W. S. Zaengl, "Can fractal features be used for recognizing 3-d partial discharge patterns," *IEEE Trans. Dielectr. Electr. Insul.*, vol. 2, no. 3, pp. 352–359, Jun. 1995. *(Check the DOI.)*
   Fractal dimension and lacunarity features.
3. **[P, seminal]** A. Contin, A. Cavallini, G. C. Montanari, G. Pasini, and F. Puletti, "Digital detection and fuzzy classification of partial discharge signals," *IEEE Trans. Dielectr. Electr. Insul.*, vol. 9, no. 3, pp. 335–348, Jun. 2002, doi: [10.1109/TDEI.2002.1007695](https://doi.org/10.1109/TDEI.2002.1007695).
   Origin of the T-F map: pulse-shape separation of sources plus Weibull features and a fuzzy classifier.
4. ★ **[P]** L. Hao and P. L. Lewin, "Partial discharge source discrimination using a support vector machine," *IEEE Trans. Dielectr. Electr. Insul.*, vol. 17, no. 1, pp. 189–197, Feb. 2010, doi: [10.1109/TDEI.2010.5412017](https://doi.org/10.1109/TDEI.2010.5412017).
   RFCT/HFCT measurements, wavelet denoising, clustering and an SVM. This sensor setup is the closest match to the project's.
5. **[P, Cable]** X. Peng, C. Zhou, D. M. Hepburn, M. D. Judd, and W. H. Siew, "Application of K-Means method to pattern recognition in on-line cable partial discharge monitoring," *IEEE Trans. Dielectr. Electr. Insul.*, vol. 20, no. 3, pp. 754–761, Jun. 2013, doi: [10.1109/TDEI.2013.6518945](https://doi.org/10.1109/TDEI.2013.6518945).
   Unsupervised recognition on on-line cable data.
6. ★ **[P, Cable]** X. Peng *et al.*, "Random forest based optimal feature selection for partial discharge pattern recognition in HV cables," *IEEE Trans. Power Del.*, vol. 34, no. 4, pp. 1715–1724, Aug. 2019, doi: [10.1109/TPWRD.2019.2918316](https://doi.org/10.1109/TPWRD.2019.2918316).
   Data: 3,500 PD and 3,500 interference pulses from artificial cable defects. Features: 34 pulse features, 119 2-D and 1,082 3-D PRPD features. RF feature selection improved both BPNN and SVM.
7. **[P]** R. Yao, J. Li, M. Hui, L. Bai, and Q. Wu, "Feature selection based on random forest for partial discharges characteristic set," *IEEE Access*, vol. 8, pp. 159151–159161, 2020, doi: [10.1109/ACCESS.2020.3019377](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2020.3019377).
8. ★ **[P, recent, Cable/XLPE]** C. Qin *et al.*, "Partial discharge signal pattern recognition of composite insulation defects in cross-linked polyethylene cables," *Sensors*, vol. 24, no. 11, Art. 3460, 2024, doi: [10.3390/s24113460](https://doi.org/10.3390/s24113460).
   VMD and entropy features with a snake-optimised SVM. Data: 4 XLPE defect classes × 600 samples. Shows feature-engineered ML remains a strong baseline.
9. **[P, recent, Cable]** W. Pan, X. Chen, and K. Zhao, "Cable-partial-discharge recognition based on a data-driven approach with optical-fiber vibration-monitoring signals," *Energies*, vol. 15, no. 15, Art. 5686, 2022, doi: [10.3390/en15155686](https://doi.org/10.3390/en15155686).
   ARMA features with a random forest gave about 98%. The sensing is optical fibre, not HFCT/UHF.
10. **[R]** W. J. K. Raymond, H. A. Illias, A. H. A. Bakar, and H. Mokhlis, "Partial discharge classifications: Review of recent progress," *Measurement*, vol. 68, pp. 164–181, 2015, doi: [10.1016/j.measurement.2015.02.032](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.measurement.2015.02.032).
11. **[R]** A. A. Mas'ud *et al.*, "Artificial neural network application for partial discharge recognition: Survey and future directions," *Energies*, vol. 9, no. 8, Art. 574, 2016, doi: [10.3390/en9080574](https://doi.org/10.3390/en9080574).
12. **[R]** ⚠ S. Lu, H. Chai, A. Sahoo, and B. T. Phung, "Condition monitoring based on partial discharge diagnostics using machine learning methods: A comprehensive state-of-the-art review," *IEEE Trans. Dielectr. Electr. Insul.*, vol. 27, no. 6, pp. 1861–1888, Dec. 2020, doi: [10.1109/TDEI.2020.009070](https://doi.org/10.1109/TDEI.2020.009070). *(Confirmed by one check but not the other; verify it on IEEE Xplore.)*

## 2.5.3 Deep-Learning Approaches
*CNNs on PRPD images and on raw pulses or time-frequency images, 1D-CNN, LSTM, CNN-LSTM, deep belief networks, transformers.*

**Foundational**
1. ★ **[P, Cable]** X. Peng *et al.*, "A convolutional neural network-based deep learning methodology for recognition of partial discharge patterns from high-voltage cables," *IEEE Trans. Power Del.*, vol. 34, no. 4, pp. 1460–1469, Aug. 2019, doi: [10.1109/TPWRD.2019.2906086](https://doi.org/10.1109/TPWRD.2019.2906086).
   Lab tests on 5 artificial defects in EPR cable with 3,500 pulses. The CNN beat SVM and BPNN. This is the key cable DL paper.
2. ★ **[P, Cable]** M. A. Khan, J. Choo, and Y.-H. Kim, "End-to-end partial discharge detection in power cables via time-domain convolutional neural networks," *J. Electr. Eng. Technol.*, vol. 14, no. 3, pp. 1299–1309, 2019, doi: [10.1007/s42835-019-00115-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s42835-019-00115-y).
   A 1D-CNN on raw waveforms: 97.38% on simulated signals and 93.23% on real ones.
3. **[P]** M.-T. Nguyen, V.-H. Nguyen, S.-J. Yun, and Y.-H. Kim, "Recurrent neural network for partial discharge diagnosis in gas-insulated switchgear," *Energies*, vol. 11, no. 5, Art. 1202, 2018, doi: [10.3390/en11051202](https://doi.org/10.3390/en11051202).
   The first LSTM applied to PRPD sequences.
4. **[P]** ⚠ H. Song, J. Dai, G. Sheng, and X. Jiang, "GIS partial discharge pattern recognition via deep convolutional neural network under complex data source," *IEEE Trans. Dielectr. Electr. Insul.*, vol. 25, no. 2, pp. 678–685, 2018. *(Check the DOI.)*
5. **[P]** M. Florkowski, "Classification of partial discharge images using deep convolutional neural networks," *Energies*, vol. 13, no. 20, Art. 5496, 2020, doi: [10.3390/en13205496](https://doi.org/10.3390/en13205496).
6. **[P]** ⚠ M. Karimi *et al.*, "A novel application of deep belief networks in learning partial discharge patterns for classifying corona, surface, and internal discharges," *IEEE Trans. Ind. Electron.*, vol. 67, no. 4, pp. 3277–3287, 2020.

**Recent cable / XLPE studies**
7. ★ **[P, Cable/XLPE]** H. I. Ückol, S. Ilhan, and A. Ozdemir, "Workmanship defect classification in medium voltage cable terminations with convolutional neural network," *Electr. Power Syst. Res.*, vol. 194, Art. 107105, 2021, doi: [10.1016/j.epsr.2021.107105](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.epsr.2021.107105).
   A CNN on PRPD images: 1,200 patterns, 5 artificial defects in 36 kV XLPE terminations. **This is closest to the project's experimental design.**
8. ★ **[P, Cable, HFCT]** C. Sun *et al.*, "Convolutional neural network-based pattern recognition of partial discharge in high-speed electric-multiple-unit cable termination," *Sensors*, vol. 24, no. 8, Art. 2660, 2024, doi: [10.3390/s24082660](https://doi.org/10.3390/s24082660).
   An HFCT and 4 artificial defects. The CNN beat BPNN and RBF networks.
9. **[P, Cable]** Y. Xie *et al.*, "Accurate identification partial discharge of cable termination for high-speed trains based on S-transform and two-dimensional convolutional network algorithm," *Sensors*, vol. 24, no. 23, Art. 7602, 2024, doi: [10.3390/s24237602](https://doi.org/10.3390/s24237602).
   S-transform time-frequency images fed to a 2D-CNN, reaching up to 98.75%.
10. ★ **[P, Cable/XLPE]** ⚠ R. Sahoo and S. Karmakar, "Effectiveness of wavelet scalogram on partial discharge pattern classification of XLPE cable insulation," *IEEE Trans. Instrum. Meas.*, vol. 73, 2024, doi: [10.1109/TIM.2024.3363790](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIM.2024.3363790).
    CWT scalogram images fed to a CNN on artificial XLPE defects. *(Check the article number.)*
11. **[P, Cable]** ⚠ O. Aldosari, M. A. Aldowsari, S. M. Batiyah, and N. Kanagaraj, "Image-based partial discharge identification in high voltage cables using hybrid deep network," *IEEE Access*, vol. 11, pp. 50325–50333, 2023.
    A hybrid CNN-LSTM with data augmentation. *(Check the DOI.)*
12. **[P, Cable]** ⚠ "Deep learning approach for cable partial discharge pattern identification," *Electrical Engineering* (Springer), 2024, doi: [10.1007/s00202-024-02571-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00202-024-02571-w).
    STFT plus a CNN on a simulated XLPE defect model (crack, contamination, void, microcrack, composite): 97.44% accuracy at 20 ms inference. *(Check the authors and volume. The simulated data is a limitation.)*

**Attention and transformers (2024–2025)**
13. ★ **[P, Cable]** Y. Li *et al.*, "Partial discharge pattern recognition based on Swin Transformer for power cable fault diagnosis in modern distribution systems," *Processes*, vol. 13, no. 3, Art. 852, 2025, doi: [10.3390/pr13030852](https://doi.org/10.3390/pr13030852).
    A lightweight Swin Transformer on PRPD from HFCT and UHF sensors: 97.52%.
14. **[P]** Y. Deng, J. Liu, K. Zhu, Q. Xie, and H. Liu, "PMSNet: Multiscale partial-discharge signal feature recognition model via a spatial interaction attention mechanism," *Sensors*, vol. 24, no. 11, Art. 3342, 2024, doi: [10.3390/s24113342](https://doi.org/10.3390/s24113342).
15. ★ **[P, Cable]** L. Chen, Q. Li, G. Long, Y. Zou, and J. Deng, "Cable partial discharge identification network based on adaptive residual diffusion denoising and morphological attention," *Sci. Rep.*, vol. 15, Art. 42848, 2025, doi: [10.1038/s41598-025-25197-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-25197-9).

**Reviews of DL for PD**
16. ★ **[R]** S. Barrios, D. Buldain, M. P. Comech, I. Gilbert, and I. Orue, "Partial discharge classification using deep learning methods—Survey of recent progress," *Energies*, vol. 12, no. 13, Art. 2485, 2019, doi: [10.3390/en12132485](https://doi.org/10.3390/en12132485).
17. ★ **[R]** S. Mantach, A. Lutfi, H. Moradi Tavasani, A. Ashraf, A. El-Hag, and B. Kordi, "Deep learning in high voltage engineering: A literature review," *Energies*, vol. 15, no. 14, Art. 5005, 2022, doi: [10.3390/en15145005](https://doi.org/10.3390/en15145005).

## 2.5.4 Comparison of Candidate Classification Methods
*Studies that benchmark ML against DL on the same dataset. Use them to justify the project's own "DL vs conventional ML" benchmark.*

| Study | Data | Compared | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Peng *et al.* 2019 (TPWRD, 2.5.3 #1) | Lab, 5 cable defects | CNN vs SVM, BPNN | CNN best, especially on similar-looking signals |
| Sun *et al.* 2024 (Sensors, 2.5.3 #8) | HFCT, 4 cable-termination defects | CNN vs BPNN, RBF | CNN best |
| Sahoo & Karmakar 2024 (TIM, 2.5.3 #10) | XLPE lab defects | Scalogram-CNN vs feature-based ML | CNN reached 100% |
| Chen *et al.* 2025 (Sci. Rep., 2.5.3 #15) | Cable PD | ARDDMA-Net vs SVM, XGBoost, BPNN, ResNet, DenseNet, Swin, MobileNet | 98.36%, at about 13% of Swin's FLOPs |
| Qin *et al.* 2024 (Sensors, 2.5.2 #8) | XLPE composite defects | SO-SVM vs BPNN, SVM | Well-engineered ML is still competitive |
| Raymond *et al.* 2021 (below) | Lab PD, clean train / noisy test | Transfer-learned CNN vs ML on manual features | CNN up to 16.9% more accurate under noise |

1. ★ **[P]** W. J. K. Raymond, C. W. Xin, L. W. Kin, and H. A. Illias, "Noise invariant partial discharge classification based on convolutional neural network," *Measurement*, vol. 177, Art. 109220, 2021, doi: [10.1016/j.measurement.2021.109220](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.measurement.2021.109220).
2. **[P]** ⚠ T.-D. Do, V.-N. Tuyet-Doan, Y.-S. Cho, J.-H. Sun, and Y.-H. Kim, "Convolutional-neural-network-based partial discharge diagnosis for power transformer using UHF sensor," *IEEE Access*, vol. 8, pp. 207377–207388, 2020.
   CNN vs ML on UHF PRPD data.
3. Plus Peng 2019, Sun 2024, Sahoo & Karmakar 2024, Chen 2025, Qin 2024 and Karimi 2020 (see above), and the reviews by Mantach 2022 and Barrios 2019.

**Comparison criteria worth discussing** (drawn from the sources above): accuracy, the amount of labelled data needed, the need for hand-crafted features, compute and inference time, robustness to noise, and interpretability.

## 2.5.5 Performance, Robustness and Generalization

**Noise robustness**
1. ★ **[P, Cable/XLPE]** W. J. K. Raymond, H. A. Illias, and A. H. A. Bakar, "Classification of partial discharge measured under different levels of noise contamination," *PLOS ONE*, vol. 12, no. 1, Art. e0170111, 2017, doi: [10.1371/journal.pone.0170111](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0170111).
   5 XLPE joints with artificial defects. Accuracy fell as noise rose for ANN, ANFIS and SVM.
2. Raymond *et al.* 2021 and Chen *et al.* 2025 (see 2.5.4).

**Generalization from lab or simulation to the field, and domain adaptation**
3. ★ **[P]** Y. Wang *et al.*, "A domain adaptive deep transfer learning method for gas-insulated switchgear partial discharge diagnosis," *IEEE Trans. Power Del.*, vol. 37, no. 4, pp. 2514–2523, 2022, doi: [10.1109/TPWRD.2021.3111862](https://doi.org/10.1109/TPWRD.2021.3111862).
4. **[P]** Y. Wu *et al.*, "Subdomain adaptation capsule network for partial discharge diagnosis in gas-insulated switchgear," *Entropy*, vol. 25, no. 5, Art. 809, 2023, doi: [10.3390/e25050809](https://doi.org/10.3390/e25050809).
   Shows the accuracy lost when a lab-trained model moves to the field; reached 93.75% on field data.
5. Khan *et al.* 2019 (2.5.3 #2): a measured sim-to-real gap for cables (97.4% simulated vs 93.2% real).

**Small datasets, class imbalance and augmentation**
6. ★ **[P]** Y. Wang *et al.*, "GAN and CNN for imbalanced partial discharge pattern recognition in GIS," *High Voltage*, vol. 7, no. 3, pp. 452–460, 2022, doi: [10.1049/hve2.12135](https://doi.org/10.1049/hve2.12135).
7. **[P]** V.-N. Tuyet-Doan *et al.*, "One-shot learning for partial discharge diagnosis using ultra-high-frequency sensor in gas-insulated switchgear," *Sensors*, vol. 20, no. 19, Art. 5562, 2020, doi: [10.3390/s20195562](https://doi.org/10.3390/s20195562).

**Multiple sources and unknown defect types**
8. ★ **[P]** B. Adam and S. Tenbohlen, "Classification of superimposed partial discharge patterns," *Energies*, vol. 14, no. 8, Art. 2144, 2021, doi: [10.3390/en14082144](https://doi.org/10.3390/en14082144).
   An LSTM reached 99% on single sources but only 43% when sources overlapped.
9. **[P]** ⚠ Y. Wang *et al.*, "Multi-source partial discharge diagnosis in gas-insulated switchgear via zero-shot learning," *Measurement*, vol. 217, Art. 113033, 2023.

**Explainability**
10. **[P]** ⚠ R. Kitani and S. Iwata, "Verification of interpretability of phase-resolved partial discharge using a CNN with SHAP," *IEEE Access*, vol. 11, 2023 (IEEE Xplore doc. 10015744).
11. **[P]** S. Mantach, P. Gill, D. R. Oliver, A. Ashraf, and B. Kordi, "An interpretable CNN model for classification of partial discharge waveforms in 3D-printed dielectric samples with different void sizes," *Neural Comput. Appl.*, vol. 34, 2022, doi: [10.1007/s00521-022-07066-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00521-022-07066-y).
12. **[P]** G. Michau, C.-C. Hsu, and O. Fink, "Interpretable detection of partial discharge in power lines with deep learning," *Sensors*, vol. 21, no. 6, Art. 2154, 2021, doi: [10.3390/s21062154](https://doi.org/10.3390/s21062154).

**Real-time and lightweight deployment** (relevant to the desirable monitoring-interface extension)
13. **[P]** L. Klein, P. Zmij, and P. Kromer, "Partial discharge detection by edge computing," *IEEE Access*, vol. 11, pp. 44192–44204, 2023, doi: [10.1109/ACCESS.2023.3268763](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2023.3268763).
14. **[P]** Y. Wang, J. Yan, Q. Sun, J. Li, and Z. Yang, "A MobileNets convolutional neural network for GIS partial discharge pattern recognition in the ubiquitous power Internet of Things context," *IEEE Access*, vol. 7, 2019, doi: [10.1109/ACCESS.2019.2946662](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2019.2946662).
15. **[P, Cable]** ⚠ "Lightweight deep learning framework for real-time PRPD-based insulation defect classification in medium-voltage cable testing," *Energies*, vol. 19, no. 9, Art. 2029, 2026, <https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/9/2029>. *(Check the authors.)*

**Note on metrics:** most PD papers report only accuracy. The team should justify reporting per-class precision, recall, F1, the confusion matrix and (for imbalanced classes) MCC or macro-F1 themselves, citing Adam & Tenbohlen 2021 and Wang *et al.* 2022 (GAN) as examples where accuracy alone hides imbalance or multi-source failure.
